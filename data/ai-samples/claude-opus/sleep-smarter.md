# Sleep Smarter by Shawn Stevenson

Most of us treat sleep as optional. When life gets busy, it's the first thing we cut. We wear exhaustion like a badge of honor and power through the day on caffeine. Shawn Stevenson, a nutritionist and host of *The Model Health Show*, wants to change that. In *Sleep Smarter*, he presents 21 practical strategies for better sleep, arguing that quality rest is the foundation for health, performance, and even weight loss.

Stevenson's interest in sleep is personal. As a young man, he was diagnosed with a degenerative bone and disc disease and told he had the spine of an 80-year-old. Through changes to his nutrition, exercise, and especially sleep, he turned his health around. That experience shapes the book's practical and encouraging tone.

## Why Sleep Matters

Stevenson opens by making the case that sleep isn't lost time. It's when your body repairs tissue, consolidates memory, balances hormones, and clears waste from the brain. Poor sleep is linked to weight gain, weakened immunity, poor concentration, mood problems, and a higher risk of chronic disease.

He emphasizes that the goal isn't just more sleep, but better sleep. Quality matters as much as quantity. Someone can spend eight hours in bed and still wake up exhausted if their sleep is fragmented or shallow.

## The 21 Strategies

The book is organized around 21 chapters, each focused on one strategy. Here are the most important themes.

### Get sunlight during the day

One of the biggest factors in good sleep is your circadian rhythm, the internal clock that regulates when you feel alert and when you feel tired. Natural light, especially in the morning, helps set that clock. Stevenson recommends getting outside in the sun early in the day to signal to your body that it's daytime, which in turn helps you feel sleepy at night.

### Avoid screens before bed

Blue light from phones, tablets, and computers suppresses melatonin, the hormone that helps you fall asleep. Stevenson suggests a "screen curfew" of at least 90 minutes before bed. If you must use devices, blue-light filters can help, but turning them off is better.

### Have a caffeine curfew

Caffeine has a long half-life and can stay in your system for many hours. Stevenson recommends cutting caffeine by around 2 p.m. so it doesn't interfere with sleep.

### Hit the sweet spot for timing

Stevenson emphasizes the importance of the hours between roughly 10 p.m. and 2 a.m., which he argues are especially valuable for hormonal recovery. Going to bed earlier and aligning your sleep with natural rhythms can improve sleep quality.

### Keep your bedroom cool

Your body temperature naturally drops at night as part of the sleep process. A cool room, ideally around 65 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit, helps support that drop. Too warm, and you're likely to sleep poorly.

### Make your room a cave

Darkness is essential. Even small amounts of light can disrupt melatonin production. Blackout curtains, covering electronics with lights, and using a sleep mask can all help.

### Make your bedroom a sanctuary

Stevenson encourages readers to use the bedroom only for sleep and sex. Working, watching TV, or scrolling in bed teaches your brain to associate the bed with alertness rather than rest.

### Exercise, at the right time

Regular exercise improves sleep quality, but timing matters. Morning workouts can help set your circadian rhythm and boost energy, while intense exercise late at night may make it harder to fall asleep for some people.

### Watch what you eat and drink

Nutrition plays a big role. Stevenson discusses foods rich in magnesium, healthy fats, and other nutrients that support sleep. He's also cautious about alcohol, which may help you fall asleep but disrupts deeper sleep stages later in the night. Eating a heavy meal right before bed can also interfere with rest.

### Fix your gut health

He connects gut health to sleep, noting that the gut produces many neurotransmitters, including serotonin, which is a precursor to melatonin. A healthy diet and good gut bacteria can improve sleep.

### Get grounded

One of the book's more unusual recommendations is "grounding" or "earthing," making direct contact with the earth by walking barefoot on grass or sand. Stevenson suggests it may help reduce stress and improve sleep. This is one of the less well-established ideas in the book, and readers may want to treat it with some skepticism.

### Calm your mind

Stress and racing thoughts are major barriers to sleep. Stevenson recommends practices like meditation, deep breathing, and journaling to quiet the mind before bed.

### Supplements and support

He discusses supplements such as magnesium, which many people may be low in. He is generally careful to frame supplements as supports rather than solutions, emphasizing lifestyle changes first.

## Key Takeaways

**Sleep is a performance tool.** Better sleep improves your health, mood, focus, and body composition.

**Light controls your clock.** Get sunlight early and avoid bright screens late.

**Environment matters.** A cool, dark, quiet bedroom used only for sleep makes a big difference.

**Timing matters.** When you sleep, eat, exercise, and drink caffeine all affect sleep quality.

**Small changes add up.** You don't need to implement all 21 strategies at once. Pick a few and build from there.

## What I Took Away

*Sleep Smarter* is easy to read and very practical. Each chapter ends with action steps, which makes it simple to try one change at a time. Most of the strategies, such as getting morning light, reducing screens at night, limiting caffeine, and keeping the bedroom dark and cool, are backed by solid research and are things nearly anyone can do.

Some of the more alternative ideas, like grounding, are less proven, and a few claims are stated more confidently than the evidence supports. But the overall message is sound and motivating.

What stuck with me most was the reframe that sleep is not lost productivity. It's an investment. The hours you spend sleeping make the rest of your hours better. After reading this book, I started getting morning sunlight and setting a firm screen curfew, and those two changes alone made a noticeable difference.

If you regularly wake up tired, struggle to fall asleep, or simply want to feel better day to day, *Sleep Smarter* is a helpful, approachable guide.
