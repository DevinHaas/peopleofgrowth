## 🚀 The Book in 3 Sentences

1. Flow is if an activity provides the optimal challenge based on your specific skills
2. The best moments occur if the body and mind is stretched to its limits in a voluntary effort
3. In a world of anxiety and depressions a person has to learn to provide rewards to herself and to find enjoyment regardless of external circumstances
  

## 🕵🏼Who Should Read It?

The books content has a broad audience since I believe that the skill of finding enoyment and challenge no matter the circumstances and without extrinsic forces is an immensily valuable skill. However since Mihály himself is a psychologist the book has some occurrences where he explains concepts on a psychological level. So I would say it is of necessity to have some level of interest in psychology and the inner workings of the mind. I would say thought that the books idiom is easy to understand and does not require any prior knowledge in those regards.
  

## ☘️ How the Book Changed Me

I already knew what Flow was and I have also experienced it many times. However I realised that I experienced it much more when I was a child. I always tried to reason about why this could be the case but without concrete success. However, after reading this book I realised that I seldomly do only one thing at a time especially now when working with AI. I let multiple agents run at the same time which requires me to context switch constantly and which hinders me to feel the Flow State. Now I am still experimenting with how to acquire flow state when working with agents since the primary purpose and advantage is to work on multiple things simultaneously. So the book changed me in the regard of that I try to optimise my process of working to achieve the focus I had when I was a child.
One thing that is to mention is that this book does not offer a todo list of actionables to follow to achieve flow but rather it explains the concept and what requirements that have to given to achieve this state. So if you are searching for a book that offers that and you are not interested in learning the process behind flow then the books can not be recommended to you.

## 📒 Summary + Notes

Mihály opens the book with talking about what happiness really is. He explains that Aristoteles has concluded already twenty-three hundered years ago that happiness is the primary thing that men and women seek. And still all these years later we hear from multiple different sources the amount of people that have depression, the number of people that commit sucide and all the others that are unhappy with there lives. Mihály addresses that and explains that we live in uncertain times, wars happening around us, technology advancements that we feel fall out of our control and the financial disparity widening to unprecedentet levels.
Mihály says that the solution to those negative forces, mostly caused by external events, is that we control the inner process of how we react to them. 


### THE ANATOMY OF CONSCIOUSNESS


Mihály starts his book with explaining what consciousness is. As he thinks that this is fundamental knowledge to understand how happiness and flow can be achieved.
He explains that the function of consciousness is the ability to capture information that is happening externally and internally and then let our body act upon the result of the evaluation of this information.
He explains that this way humans have developed the ability to change a persons emotions decoupled by a persons internal or external informations.
Without consciousness we would react in a reflexiv manner to our sensations and we would not be able to tell lies, invent information, day dream and create new innovativ scientific theories.

Interestingly Mihály said that there is no scientific branch that covers consciousness directly which is why there is no single description of how it works.
Also I found very inspiring that although we know that consciousness acts similar to a mirror for external and internal events it is only a very precise selection of those events and lays upon them a reality of its own.
That means that although we know that there are things happening outside of our consciousness we have no direct evidence of everything that happens outside of it.
One thing that we should not forget when talking about consciousness is the fact that it is limited. 
Our nervous system has a limited capability of capturing attention and serving it to our consciousness which means that it is very important that we can control what we let into our consciousness and what we should avoid. 
Mihály references this as *Psychic Energy* or *Attention* and he uses this through out the book as almost a currency of some sort. You have it but if you spend it is gone.
Now he explains later in the book that you can regenerate and regain Psychic Energy with specific practices and moments of relaxations but the fact that it is limited stands.
One thing that I found very interesting and I opening is that the outcome and content of ones live is the result of where the person has focused and invested her attention.
Obviously that makes total sense and is not hard to understand but it is nonetheless something that caught my eye because it is simple yet so complex to do right.

The last important thing that the author covers is the concept of *Psychic Entropy* or the disorder of consciousness.
The concept of psychic Entropy is explained pretty simple by the book. It is when information appears in consciousness that conflicts with a persons current goal.
Now the outcome of preventing this disorder happening is *Flow*.

### ENJOYMENT AND THE QUALITY OF LIFE

Mihály mentions to ways to control ones quality of life:
- The first is to try making external conditions match our goals. 
- The second is to change how we experience external conditions to make them fit our goals better.
He also mentions that neither of these ways is very effective on its own. 

In the book we get the example of wanting security. Even though we can buy our own private island that is far away from any threat and with the best security it will not necesseraly give us an inner sense of freedom.

### Pleasure and enjoyment

This distinction I found quite interesting. Mihály says that pleasure is important to life. But he explains that it is more about maintaining order. If we feel hungry we want to eat and when we than follow that desire and satisfy it we feel pleasure. Now one thing, Mihály says, is not possible to be achieved by pleasure and that is new order in consciousness. For that we need enjoyable events. Enjoyment, as Mihály describes it, is a forward movement. Something that goes further than just the meer satisfaction. It is novelty, excitement and achievement.
I found this analogy quite interesting and for me both things where the same thing but the more I thought about this the more I started agreeing with Mihály.
This fundamental necessity for learning and improving makes so much sense for humans also from a biological standpoint.
If we look at children at a young age almost all their actions provide a challenge and with that excitement, novality and eventually achievement. 
Now one thing that Mihály also covered is why we he thinks that in adolesence we loose this connection between growth and enjoyment and he says that school plays a big part in this. We impose external learning goals on the children which leads to the loss of this excitement.

One thing that Mihály also mentioned in this chapter what components are contained in enjoyment. And I wanted to have those listed in this summary:
- **First**, the experience usually occurs when we confront tasks we have a chance of completing. 
- **Second**, we must be able to concentrate on what we are doing. 
- **Third** and **fourth**, the concentration is usually possible because the task undertaken has clear goals and provides immediate feedback. 
- **Fifth**, one acts with a deep but effortless involvement that removes from awareness the worries and frustrations of everyday life. 
- **Sixth**, enjoyable experiences allow people to exercise a sense of control over their actions. 
- **Seventh**, concern for the self disappears, yet paradoxically the sense of self emerges stronger after the flow experience is over. 
- **Eigth**, the sense of the duration of time is altered; hours pass by in minutes, and minutes can stretch out to seem like hours.

#### The autotelic experience

This I find amazing and I think it is such a nice beproduct of a enjoyable experience with clear goals and feedback. It is the fact that the element of an optimal experience is that it becomes a end to it self. Although we have specified a clear goal the activity becomes intrinsically rewarding.
Autotelic experiences are a synonym for flow. When we have a autotelic experience we have reached the flow state.


### The Conditions of Flow

To reach the flow state there are specific requirements that have to be met.
Mihály starts the chapter first with introducing us in the works of Roger Caillois a french psychological anthropologist.
He has looked at the games that are played in human history and divided them into 4 categories

**Agon** includes games that have competition as their main feature, such as most sports and athletic events; **alea** is the class that includes all games of chance, from dice to bingo; **ilinx**, or **vertigo**, is the name he gives to activities that alter consciousness by scrambling ordinary perception, such as riding a merry-go-round or skydiving; and **mimicry** is the group of activities in which alternative realities are created, such as dance, theater, and the arts in general.

Now as Mihály found in his study there was one thing that all flow experiences have in common no matter which type of experience they provide (*agon, alea, vertigo or mimicry*).
The lift the individual to a new level of complexity. That is of mind or body.

In both states, that is anxiety and boredom, we don't feel happy and we are motivated to reach the flow channel again.
When we feel anxiety we have the option of reducing the challenge or actually improving our skills.
When we are bored we have to increase the challenge to reach the flow channel again.
Additional to the fact that to reach flow we need the correct mixture of challenge and skill we also need the ability to control our consciousness.
Mihály mentions the fact that there are people with genetical disadvantages in the area of focus and there are external factors which can increase or decrease a persons ability to focus.

### Work as flow

The same principles that hold in other areas and domains also hold in the workplace. We need a goal, feedback, focus and some sort of improvement happening.
But that is not why I thought this chapter is interesting. Because one thing Mihály covered in an other study of his is the difference of leisure time and work time. What he found is astonishing.
People experience less flow in their leisure time than during their work time and still the majority wants to have more leisure time. Something that can be connected here is that most people think that their psychic energy that they invest in the job does not pay towards their life goals although this contradicts with study that show that a majority of people is quite happy with their current job.
Mihály explains this paradoxon with the following conclusion:
when it comes to work, people do not heed the evidence of their senses. They disregard the quality of immediate experience, and base their motivation instead on the strongly rooted cultural stereotype of what work is supposed to be like. They think of it as an imposition, a constraint, an infringement of their freedom, and therefore something to be avoided as much as possible.

I found this very interesting and Mihály's explanation of why people don't feel flow in the work place makes total sense. 
Leisure time is unstructured and needs greater effort to create an optimal experience.
In the workplace we have more external rules set upon us, more rules and less options which makes it easier for us to create an autotelic experience.
### The making of meaning

This is a very nice chapter and I like that Mihály has added this add the end of his book since it is an important question and it is very tightly related to flow. Considering the fact that ideally we want our flow experiences to give us a meaning in life or at least to enable use to create meaning in life.
In the book there are 3 senses of meaing that are mentioned:
- **1**: The first one points to an end, purpose or significance of something as in "What is the meaning of life"? People who find their lives meaningful usually have a goal that is challenging enough to take up all their energies, a goal that can give significance to their lives.
- **2**: The second sense of the word meaning refers to the expression of intentionality. It is not enough to find a purpose that unifies one’s goals; one must also carry through and meet its challenges. The purpose must result in strivings; intent has to be translated into action. We may call this resolution in the pursuit of one’s goals.
- **3**: The third sense is when **1** and **2** is achieved simultatiously. When an important goal is pursued with resolution, and all one’s varied activities fit together into a unified flow experience, the result is that harmony is brought to consciousness.

That means we can summarise meaning as:
Creating meaning involves bringing order to the contents of the mind by integrating one’s actions into a unified flow experience.
