## 🚀 The Book in 3 Sentences

1. Remember that you can always go native later
2. Having multiple projects at the same time can be good, but the main one should get 80% of the invested time
3. Build your idea with the tools you know

## 🕵🏼Who Should Read It?

This book has a very niche readership: builders and indie developers who want to scale a side hustle and maybe be able to live off it later on.
Since the book starts from the basics, and Pieter Levels himself tailored it for beginners in this space, it can be cumbersome for more experienced readers.

## ☘️ How the Book Changed Me
This book did not really change me, but it gave me some very clear instructions to follow when launching a business on Product Hunt or similar sites. It taught me that organic growth is much better than superficial growth. It also strengthened my opinion on really building something that users want and need, something that has meaning and the possibility to change lives in a morally correct way.

## 📒 Summary + Notes

This book is structured more like a collection of notes than a classical book.
It is meant to be used as a reference. It is also not meant to be read in one go, but rather multiple times, reading only the parts that are currently applicable.

One thing that Pieter Levels mentions is that it is completely normal to have 10 - 30 products running for one to three years before you have anything that actually works and that you can live off.
Something that I realised in my own life and with my own projects, and that Pieter also talks about in his book, is the importance of solving your own problems.
When we try to solve other people's problems, we always have to make guesses, and this can lead to bad decisions and mistakes.
If we ourselves are the customers, we know what we want and can build it to solve our own pain.
In addition to that, we are the greatest experts on our own problems, which can be a huge benefit when trying to think of solutions for them.
One thing that is very important here is to really start from a problem and not jump directly to a solution.
This is something that is currently happening all over the place.
People are implementing AI in each and every part of software even though the problem does not call for it.

One thing that I liked is that Pieter Levels breaks down what it takes to earn $1 million in a year.

Something else that I found interesting is that Pieter emphasises starting in a micro niche and finding fit in this niche first before scaling.

**Your enemy is perfection**
This is something that I struggle with a lot, since I hate publishing unfinished or imperfect things.
I tend to iterate on the smallest details for far too long, which can lead to inaction.
But I like that Pieter emphasises this and explains that new users are quite okay with finding bugs as long as you fix them afterwards and listen to their feedback.

**Where to launch**
- *Product Hunt*: Widely used and can generate quick traffic. The timing of a launch on Product Hunt is quite important. Product Hunt's timezone is San Francisco (PST) and its ranking resets at midnight. That means you need to launch at 00:00:01 or not much later. Pieter also says that he immediately jumps into the Product Hunt comments and writes an introduction post about who he is, why he made the app and what his future plans for it are.
- *Hacker News*: Good for getting honest feedback, but you have to be ready for very harsh feedback. Pieter says that it is important to make the titles personal:
  "Show HN: I made a site that lets you subscribe to pet food delivery" instead of "Petsy.com - The best food delivery for pets"
- *Reddit*: It is more mainstream and can generate a lot of traffic, so the site has to be ready for that.


**Things not to do**
- Asking people to share/like/post your product
- Buying fake upvotes, likes or followers. The same applies to buying fake virality


**Get Press**
This is interesting, since I never considered contacting the press myself about an app launch or project.
Pieter emphasises that it is important to find specific journalists who could be interested in one's project or idea and to write personal messages to them directly.
It is very important that everything is organic and natural. People love authenticity.

**How to achieve organic growth**
- Build in public
- Tell stories to people and the press
- Keep launching (multiple versions)
- Make it easy for people to share
- Get feedback from users
