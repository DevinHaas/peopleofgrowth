## 📒 Summary + Notes

This book is almost structured like a summary of notes instead of a classical structure of a book.
It is thought to be used as a research medium. Also it is not thought to be read in one go but rather multiple times an only the specific parts that are applicable currently.

One thing that Pieter Levels mentions is that it is completely normal to have 10 - 30 products running for 1 to 3 years before you have anything that actually works and you can live of.
Something that I realised in my own life and with my own projects but that also Pieter talks about in his book is the importance of solving his own problems. 
When we try to solve problems of other people we always have to make guesses and this can lead to bad decisions and mistakes.
If we ourself's are our customers we know what we want and can  build it to solve our own pain.
Additional to that comes that we are the greatest expert in our own problems which can be a huge benefit when trying to think of solutions for those problems.
One thing that is very important here is to really start from a problem and not directly jump to a solution.
This is something that happens currently all over the place.
People are implementing AI in each and every part of software even though the problem is not specifically asking for it.

Something else that I found interesting is that Pieter emphasises to start in a mikro nieche and to first fit to this nieche before scaling.

**Your enemy is perfection**
This is something that I struggle with a lot since I hate publishing and releasing unfinished or imperfect things.
I tend to iterate on the smallest details for far to long which can lead to inaction. 
But I like that Pieter emphasises this and explains that new users are quite okay to find bugs as longs as you fix them afterwards and listen to their feedback

**Where to launch**
- *Product Hunt*: Widely used and can generate quick traffic. The timing of a launch on Product Hunt is quite important. Product Hunt's timezone is San Francisco (PST timezone) and its ranking resets at midnight. That means you will need to launch at 00:00:01 or not much later. Pieter also says that he immediately jumps in to the Product Hunt comments and writes an introduction post about who himselfs, why he made this app and what his future plan is with it
- *Hacker News*: Good to get honest feedback but you have to be ready to get very harsh feedback. Pieter says that it is important to make the titles personal:
  "Show HN: I made a site that lets you subscribe to pet food delivery" instead of " Petsy.com - The best food delivery for pets"
- *Reddit*: It is more mainstream and it can generate a lot of traffic. So the site has to be ready for that


**Things not to do**
- Asking people to share/like/post your product 
- Buying fake upvotes, likes, followers The same applies to buying fake virality


**Get Press**
This is interesting since I never considered contacting press my self about an app launch or project.
Pieter emphasises that it is important to find specific journalists that could be interested in ones project or idea and to write personal messages to them directly.
It is very imporant that everything is organic and natural. People love authenticity.

**How to achieve organic growth**
- Build in public
- Tell stories to people and press
- keep launching (multiple version)
- Make people share it easily
- Get feedback from users
